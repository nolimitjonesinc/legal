CCB Replies — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 15 September 2026  ·  Version 2.2
In short: CCB Replies is a workplace tool used by a company's own communications staff. It processes content that people have already published publicly, so staff can find relevant conversations and draft replies to them. Every reply requires human approval before it is published. The Extension does not seek private account information, credentials, browsing history, or any non-public information about members of the public. It does not sell data to anyone, and — unless the company deliberately switches on Team mode — everything stays on the staff member's own computer.

1. Who this policy is for

CCB Replies ("the Extension") is a Chrome extension supplied by Danny Jones ("we", "the vendor") to businesses for use by their own employees. It is not a consumer product and is not marketed to the general public.

Two groups are relevant here: the staff member who installs and uses the Extension, and the company that deploys it and decides how it is configured. Where the company controls a decision, this policy says so plainly.

2. What the Extension does

The Extension runs inside the staff member's own browser, on the sites listed below, while that staff member is signed in under their own account. It:

  • reads the text of posts, comments and reviews that are already publicly visible on the page, together with their public display name or handle, the page address, and publicly shown details such as the community, rating or post age;
  • scores those posts against the company's own configured topics of interest;
  • sends selected post text to an AI model to draft a suggested reply;
  • shows that draft to a staff member for review and editing;
  • on approval, places the approved text into the site's own reply box, or copies it to the clipboard;
  • records what was drafted, edited and sent in an activity log.

The sites it runs on are: Reddit, YouTube, Google Business Reviews (Maps, Search and Local review panels), and the customer's own community forum where configured.

Posting behaviour

On most sites the Extension fills the reply box and stops — the staff member clicks the site's own Post button. On old.reddit.com only, the company may enable an option in which the Extension clicks the site's Submit button on the staff member's behalf, and only after that staff member has explicitly approved that specific reply. There is no unattended, scheduled or bulk posting of any kind. The Extension never posts without a human having approved the exact wording first.

3. What data is handled, and where it goes

Solo mode (the default)

Everything the Extension keeps — the queue of flagged posts, drafts, settings, the activity log — is stored in Chrome's own extension storage on the staff member's computer. The only data that leaves the browser is:

  • the text of a public post plus the drafting instructions, sent to the configured AI provider so it can generate a suggestion;
  • ordinary page requests to the public site being viewed, made under the staff member's own logged-in session.

No vendor-operated server receives any of this in Solo mode. The vendor cannot see it.

Team mode (optional, switched on by a company administrator)

When a company turns on Team mode, the same flagged posts, drafts and sent replies are also written to a Postgres database hosted on Supabase, in a project the company itself provisions, owns and controls. Records written include the post title, an excerpt of the post text, the page address, the AI-drafted reply, the final reply text sent, the staff account that sent it, and a timestamp. The poster's display name or handle is not written to the database; it stays in the reviewer's own browser, where it is used to show who the reply is addressed to. Access is restricted by database-enforced row-level rules tied to each staff member's assigned role.

Team accounts are identified by work email address and are created by a company administrator. The vendor does not operate that database and holds no independent copy of its contents.

4. The AI provider

Reply drafting and post scoring are performed by a large language model. By default this is Anthropic's Claude API. A company whose IT policy requires it may instead configure its own Microsoft Azure OpenAI deployment, in which case prompts go to that company's own cloud tenant and not to Anthropic.

Anthropic currently states that inputs and outputs submitted through its commercial API are not used to train its models by default, and that they are normally deleted from its backend within 30 days — subject to exceptions including separately agreed retention arrangements, legal obligations, and enforcement of its usage policies. That is Anthropic's stated policy, which Anthropic may change, and not a guarantee made by the vendor.

Where a company supplies a single shared AI key, that key is held server-side in the company's own Supabase project and is never present in any staff member's browser.

5. What the Extension never does

  • It does not collect payment details, and there is no consumer sign-up.
  • It does not sell or rent data, and does not disclose data to data brokers, advertisers or any third party for advertising or profiling. Public post content is transmitted only to the AI provider selected for the drafting function and, in Team mode, to infrastructure the deploying company controls.
  • It does not access the browser's history, and it does not run on, read or inject anything into any site other than those listed in section 2. To find the right tab when a reviewer opens a queue item or runs a self-test, it briefly checks the addresses of the tabs currently open; it keeps none of them, transmits none of them, and looks at nothing inside a tab that is not one of the listed sites.
  • It does not capture account credentials, cookies or session tokens from any site.
  • It does not attempt to determine a poster's real-world identity, enrich a public profile with information from anywhere else, or match a poster against internal patient, customer, marketing or other private records. It collects no information about a member of the public beyond what that person has already published on the public page being viewed.
  • It does not send data to the vendor. The vendor operates no server that receives customer content.

6. Sensitive subject matter

The Extension may be used in health-adjacent communities, including fertility discussion forums. It reads only what a person has chosen to publish publicly.

The Extension is designed for general, supportive, non-clinical replies. It includes guidance intended to steer drafts away from medical advice, away from an individual's specific circumstances, and away from any information not already visible in that person's own public post. These are design features, not guarantees: every draft is reviewed and approved by the deploying company's own staff, and that company decides what is actually published.

The Extension is not intended for use as a medical device, is not designed to receive or store patient records, and is not supplied for any clinical purpose.

Information flows one way. The Extension reads public web pages and writes reply text; it never reads from the company's internal systems. It holds no credentials for, and makes no connection to, electronic health records, patient portals, customer databases, marketing platforms or CRM systems, and no information from any of those systems is available to it or to the AI model. Nothing a reviewer sees in the queue comes from anywhere but the public page itself.

Link tagging, described in section 7, works in the opposite direction and does not change this: the company's own website and CRM may record visits arriving from a tagged link, in the company's own systems and under its own privacy notice. The Extension sends nothing to them and receives nothing from them.

7. Link tagging

Link tagging is off until a company turns it on. When it is on, links that come from the company's own approved resource library are stamped with standard UTM campaign parameters at the moment a reply is approved. Links the reviewer types themselves, and links already present in the conversation, are left alone.

The parameters describe the campaign and the context of the reply: the campaign name and source the company types in itself, the platform the reply was published on, and a reference number for the queue item the reply came from. That reference lets the company trace a visit back to the conversation that prompted it. It says nothing about who the visitor is, and no cookie, pixel, device fingerprint or personal identifier is added by the Extension.

What happens after someone clicks is the company's own business, not the Extension's. A tagged link is an ordinary web address. When a person clicks it they arrive at the company's own website, and whatever that website and the company's analytics or CRM normally record about a visit — which may over time be associated with a person who later identifies themselves, for example by filling in a form — is recorded in the company's own systems under the company's own privacy notice. The Extension is not involved in that, sends nothing to any CRM or analytics platform, holds no CRM credentials, and never learns who clicked.

8. Retention and deletion

  • Solo mode: data stays in the browser until the staff member clears it or uses an in-product reset. Data held in Chrome's local extension storage — the queue, drafts and activity log — is removed when the Extension is uninstalled. Settings held in Chrome Sync may already have been synchronised to the staff member's other signed-in Chrome browsers through their Google account, and are removed according to Chrome's own sync behaviour rather than instantly everywhere.
  • Team mode: data is retained in the company's own database under the company's own retention policy. A company administrator can delete records directly at any time.

9. Requests about personal data

The deploying company generally determines why and how content is processed through the Extension, and is responsible for handling privacy requests relating to that use. Any request about personal data handled through the Extension should therefore be directed to that company. The vendor does not maintain an independent database containing customer content and is not in a position to answer such requests on the company's behalf. Where a formal controller and processor relationship applies, it is set out in the agreement between the vendor and the company rather than assumed by this policy.

10. Security

  • All network communication uses HTTPS.
  • No AI provider key is embedded in the published Extension code.
  • Team data access is enforced by database row-level security tied to the signed-in account's role.
  • The Extension loads no remote code; all executable code ships inside the reviewed package.

11. Chrome Web Store Limited Use

CCB Replies' use and transfer of information received through Chrome complies with the Chrome Web Store User Data Policy, including its Limited Use requirements. Information the Extension accesses is used only to provide and improve the workplace reply-assistance functionality described in this policy. It is not used for advertising, for profiling unrelated to that functionality, for creditworthiness or lending decisions, or for sale to third parties, and it is not transferred to data brokers or information resellers. Any human reading of that information is limited to what a customer's own staff do inside the product, to what a user explicitly asks the vendor to look at for support, and to what is required by law or for security.

12. Children

The Extension is a workplace tool for adult employees and is not directed at anyone under 18.

13. Changes

This policy may be updated as the product changes. Material changes will be reflected in the "Last updated" date above, and customers under a current agreement will be notified.
